Keep it beautiful

The Handbag Care Guide

Simple, safe routines for cleaning, conditioning and storing fine leather bags, plus what to do when accidents happen.

The golden rules

Gentle, little and often

Most damage to handbags comes from too much care, not too little: harsh cleaners, soaking, heavy conditioning or aggressive rubbing. Leather needs a light touch.

  1. Always spot test any product on a hidden area and wait 24 hours.
  2. Use products made for leather, never household sprays, baby wipes or alcohol.
  3. Less is more. A pea-sized amount of conditioner is enough for most bags.
  4. Let it breathe. Leather needs airflow; plastic covers trap moisture and can cause mould.
  5. Know when to stop. Deep stains, colour loss and torn stitching belong with a professional.

First aid

When accidents happen

Rain

Blot, do not rub, with a soft towel. Stuff with tissue and let it dry naturally at room temperature, away from radiators and hairdryers.

Ink

Act quickly but gently. Blot any wet ink, then take the bag to a leather specialist. Home remedies often spread the stain.

Oil or grease

Blot the excess and leave it; small oil marks on smooth leather often fade as they absorb. For larger marks, seek professional help.

Scratches

Light scratches on smooth leather can often be softened by rubbing gently with a clean fingertip. Deeper scratches may need recolouring by a specialist.

Storage

How to store a bag between seasons

  • Empty every pocket and wipe the interior clean.
  • Stuff lightly with acid-free tissue or a bag pillow to hold the shape.
  • Wrap chains and metal hardware in tissue so they do not mark the leather.
  • Place the bag in a breathable cotton or flannel dust bag.
  • Store upright on a shelf, with space around it, away from sunlight and heat.
  • Take it out every few months to air and check its condition.

Care advice is general. Always follow any specific instructions from the maker of your bag.
